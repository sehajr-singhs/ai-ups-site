Certified grid-side behavior of an AI UPS

A proof that an AI training campus behind a battery UPS shows the grid a bounded ramp and a steady voltage, for every load in range, on a public model.

5Fleet results (Paper 2)

Paper 1 covers one campus. Paper 2 puts several units on the Kundur two-area test system (two sites, three 100 MW campuses each) and asks them to damp inter-area oscillations and support frequency after a generator trip. The per-unit battery certificate still holds with a grid-service command added, up to 15 MW of headroom with a 20 MW authority. Paper 2 uses sourced values where they exist: R = 20 MW/min and one-way efficiency 0.927.

6Blueprint (Paper 3)

Paper 3 turns the proved requirements into a paper design for a grid-forming UPS on a 100 MW campus with a 20 MW/min ramp limit. Nothing was built or tested.

The shunt design cannot ride through voltage sags; a series path would cost more ($7.31M converter against $4.90M) and is not certified. The cell is a 2.5 Ah power cell chosen because its datasheet was in hand, so a real pack would change count and cost. Paper 3.

7What this does not show

  • The parameters are assumed. The load range, ramp limit, efficiency and feeder are round values, not from a real site.
  • Averaged power only. No inverter current or PLL dynamics, switching, delay or measurement noise.
  • No faults, protection or ride-through. Loads outside 60 to 100 MW, such as a full campus trip, are not covered.
  • The proofs are on simplified models: a stiff source behind one line, and in Paper 2 a linear or piecewise-affine network. The nonlinear nadir sits 0.07 to 0.11 Hz below the verified bound.
  • The branch-and-bound code is tested, not formally verified.
  • The IEEE 39-bus run did not finish, so there is no 39-bus result.
  • Paper 3 is a paper design. Nothing was built or measured.
  • The cost is a floor. It leaves out transformers, cooling and buildings, and uses 2021 grid-storage prices.
  • On this assumed stiff feeder, the voltage benefit is removing steps (1.65% for a full load step without the UPS), not the static band.

8What I'd need to take it further

  • Real ramp limits. Actual interconnection limits and measurement windows for the sites in question, in place of the 10 and 20 MW/min used here.
  • Battery sizing. Real cell, pack and converter data, including efficiency and derating, to replace the assumed values.
  • Load traces. Recorded campus power traces to test the load range and swing shapes, and to replace the symmetric trapezoid used for the supercapacitor sizing.
